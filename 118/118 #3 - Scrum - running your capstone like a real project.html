SDGKU FSDITopic 118.3
Why this matters

Weeks of coding with nothing to show

The risk

Students plan once, then code for weeks without checkpoints.

What usually happens

Work drifts to the fun parts, not the demo.

The result

The last week becomes a panic sprint.

Short cycles with visible outcomes keep the capstone honest.

A framework you can reuse

The scrum loop, solo edition

1

Product backlog

Everything, ordered by user value.

2

Sprint

A short timebox with one clear goal.

3

Stand-up

Plan, touchpoint, evidence — daily.

4

Review / demo

Show working software, not slides.

5

Retrospective

Change one thing about how you work.

Examples you’ll recognize

Sprint goals that fit a capstone

Store

“A customer can order one product end to end.”

Booking

“A visitor can request and confirm a slot.”

Community

“A user can post and get a reply.”

Each goal is a complete, demoable slice — not a layer.

SDGKU FSDITopic 118.3
Using AI as a design partner, not autopilot

AI grooms the backlog — you own the goal

Good use

  • Ask AI to break a feature into backlog items with acceptance criteria
  • Ask it to size items (S/M/L) and flag risky ones
  • Don’t let AI set your sprint goal — that’s a product decision
!

Guardrail

The backlog is ordered by user value — not by what is fun to code.

SDGKU FSDITopic 118.3
Talk it through

Discussion prompts

1 What is your sprint goal for this week?
2 Which backlog item is riskiest — and is it early or late in the plan?
3 What did your last “demo” actually show?

Studio task

Set up your one-person scrum

  • Write your backlog (10+ items, ordered by value).
  • Pick a one-week sprint goal and mark the 3 items that achieve it.
  • Schedule your Friday demo — what will you show, to whom?
